The firmware side of the platform. You are writing an Electrosmith Daisy
program: **libDaisy** and **DaisySP** do the work they always did, and
`rodeo-sdk` adds what the Daisy cannot do on its own — the connectors, the
satellite chain, the second codec, and a pile of logic that is easier to test on
a laptop than on a bench.

:::caution[Being rewritten, in the open]
The boards were ordered on 18 October 2026 and none has been powered on. The SDK
is mid-rewrite to match them, so this documents a library in motion rather than
a finished one. Each page says what is written and what is not; the plan is
`planning/REV_B_PLAN.md`.
:::

## What works today

- **USB.** `examples/usb_device` turns a bare Seed into a composite USB device on
  TinyUSB: stereo audio in and out at 48 kHz / 24-bit, USB-MIDI, a serial port
  and a RAM disk, all at once. Measured full-duplex loopback came back
  glitch-free over sixty seconds at 31 ms round trip. This replaces libDaisy's
  own USB stack.
- **The [pure-logic headers](/sdk/reference/pure-logic/).** Scaling, filtering,
  UI smoothing, the scope — no hardware in them, tested on a laptop, and
  unaffected by the board revision.
- **[Toolchain and template](/sdk/install/).** Building, flashing and starting an
  app have not changed.

## What is still being written

The satellite-chain driver, the second codec on SAI2, a new `board.h` for the Rev
B pin map, and the bring-up examples that go with them. The satellite firmware
will be its own top-level tree built with ch32v003fun — one image for every
satellite kind, with an ID resistor choosing which.

## How it is put together

**Layer 1 is pure logic.** Headers with no hardware in them at all, so they build
and run on a laptop under `common/tests`. If something can be tested without a
board, it belongs here.

**Layer 2 is the board.** Drivers that talk to real silicon over SPI, I²C and
UART. They depend on `board.h`, which is the single source of truth for the pin
map.

**Layer 3 is your instrument.** The control assignments — which knob is cutoff,
which encoder picks the waveform — live in your app and its own control map, not
in the SDK. If something here names a specific knob or a specific sound, it is in
the wrong place.
