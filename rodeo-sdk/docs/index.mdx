The firmware side of the platform. You are writing an Electrosmith Daisy
program: **libDaisy** and **DaisySP** do the work they always did, and
`rodeo-sdk` adds what the Daisy cannot do on its own — the connectors, the
satellite chain, the second codec, and a pile of logic that is easier to test on
a laptop than on a bench.

:::caution[Being rewritten, in the open]
The first Rev B boards arrived on 28 October 2026 and most of this has now run
on one. The SDK is still mid-rewrite, so this documents a library in motion
rather than a finished one. Each page says what is written, what has run on
hardware and what has not; the plan and its bring-up table are
`planning/REV_B_PLAN.md`.
:::

## What works today

- **USB on the panel USB-C,** which is the whole reason Rev B exists. On a Rev B
  board: UAC1 four in and four out at 16-bit, USB-MIDI, a serial port, and the
  SD card as a drive in disk mode. Tested on macOS; Windows and Linux have not
  been tried. 24-bit audio works on a direct port but not behind a USB 2.0 hub,
  so 16-bit is the default.
- **Both codecs, in one callback.** `audio_4x4` runs four in and four out across
  the Seed's codec and the CS4272. The second codec's ADC is noisy while its own
  DAC plays — a board fault, not a driver one, and written up on
  [the motherboard's page](/ecosystem/rodeo-motherboard/).
- **The satellite chain, end to end.** A ring of a pot bank and an encoder bank
  ran about 24,600 scans in two minutes with none bad, every control read and
  every LED driven.
- **The SD card** in 1-bit mode, 388 KB/s write and 1.24 MB/s read on a 2 GB
  card, and the same card mounted on a Mac in disk mode.
- **The [pure-logic headers](/sdk/reference/pure-logic/).** Scaling, filtering,
  UI smoothing, the scope — no hardware in them, tested on a laptop, and
  unaffected by the board revision. Every function and type is in the
  [API reference](/sdk/reference/api/pot-ui/), generated from the headers.
- **[The satellite chain's frame format](/sdk/reference/api/sat-frame/).**
  `sat_frame.h` is the wire protocol in code, shared by the Seed and the
  satellites and tested by playing both, down to a simulated ring.
- **[The I/O connectors](/sdk/guides/io-connectors/).** Digital, analog in and
  CV out on the ten connectors, checked against what each one can do, and
  tested on a bare Seed wired to itself.
- **[The Rev B pin map](/sdk/guides/board/).** `board.h` names every pin by what
  it is wired to, and host tests check it against libDaisy's own pin table.
- **[Toolchain and template](/sdk/install/).** Building, flashing and starting an
  app have not changed.

## What is still being written

USB as a library rather than an example, and the guide pages for the drivers
that landed during bring-up. The battery gauge has a driver and host tests but
has never answered with a real number: the cell on our board was plugged in
backwards and took the gauge with it.

Each of these gets its page here when its code lands. `rodeo-demo` is the first
instrument being written against this SDK; rodeo-002 moves onto it after.

## How it is put together

**Layer 1 is pure logic.** Headers with no hardware in them at all, so they build
and run on a laptop under `common/tests`. If something can be tested without a
board, it belongs here.

**Layer 2 is the board.** Drivers that talk to real silicon over SPI, I²C and
UART. They depend on `board.h`, which is the single source of truth for the pin
map.

**Layer 3 is your instrument.** The control assignments — which knob is cutoff,
which encoder picks the waveform — live in your app and its own control map, not
in the SDK. If something here names a specific knob or a specific sound, it is in
the wrong place.
