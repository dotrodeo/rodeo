---
title: Satellite chain
description: The wire contract for Rodeo's control satellites — connector, line rate, how the ring closes itself, frame layout and slot kinds.
---

The chain is the reason a Rodeo panel can grow without the motherboard changing.
Each satellite carries a cent-scale microcontroller, reads its own controls, and
passes frames along a ring. **A satellite's position in the chain is its
address**, so there is nothing to configure and no jumpers to set.

This page is the short version. The contract in full — discovery, commands,
fault codes, programming — is
`hardware/rodeo-motherboard/docs/RODEO_SATELLITE_CHAIN_CONTRACT.md`.

## Connector

Every satellite has an **IN** and an **OUT**, both **JST SH 5-pin**. The
motherboard has one chain connector wired like a satellite's OUT.

| Pin | Signal | On a satellite |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GND | — |
| 2 | 3V3 | Supply in, passed straight through to OUT |
| 3 | `DATA_FWD` | MCU RX on IN, MCU TX on OUT |
| 4 | `DATA_RET` | Straight through, with the MCU's return tap on it |
| 5 | `PRESENT` | Tied to GND on IN; an input with a pull-up on OUT |

`DATA_FWD` is point to point — one transmitter and one receiver per cable.
`DATA_RET` is a single wire from the end of the chain back to the Seed, passing
through every satellite.

## Line

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | UART, 8N1, LSB first, idle high |
| Rate | **1 Mbaud** |
| Levels | 3.3 V CMOS; satellites run directly from the chain supply |
| Seed side | USART3 — TX on pin 3, RX on pin 2 |

## The ring closes itself

At boot and every millisecond after, each satellite reads the `PRESENT` pin on
its OUT connector.

- **Low** — something is plugged in downstream, so transmit on `DATA_FWD`.
- **High** — this is the last satellite, so move the UART's transmit onto the
  `DATA_RET` tap. Frames then travel back along the return wire to the Seed.

No terminator plug, and nothing to configure when a panel grows. A satellite
drives the return tap only while it is actually transmitting; the rest of the
time the tap is high-impedance and the motherboard's pull-up holds the line
idle, so two satellites can never fight over the return wire.

## Frames

A 4-byte header followed by N slots of 4 bytes, at most 260 bytes — 2.6 ms at
1 Mbaud.

**Header**

| Byte | Field |
|---|---|
| 0 | `SOF`, `0xA5` for protocol v1 |
| 1 | `TYPE` — `0x01` scan, `0x02` discover, `0x03` command |
| 2 | `N`, slots that follow, 0–64 |
| 3 | `HOP`, satellites passed through so far |

**Slot** — `KIND`, `D0`, `D1`, then a CRC-8 over the first three bytes
(polynomial `0x07`, init `0x00`, CRC-8/SMBUS).

## Slot kinds

Satellites **report state, not events**. If a frame is lost nothing is missed:
counters wrap, and the Seed works out what changed from the difference with the
last good value.

| `KIND` | Name | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| `0x01` | `POT` | 16-bit position, scaled 0–65535 whatever the ADC's resolution |
| `0x02` | `BUTTON` | Pressed-now bit, plus a press counter that wraps at 256 |
| `0x03` | `ENCODER` | Detent counter wrapping at 256, plus switch state and its counter |
| `0x04` | `SWITCH` | Position index |
| `0x7F` | `FAULT` | Fault code and detail |
| `0x10` | `LED` | Seed to satellite: brightness, and steady or blinking |

## Board kinds

A resistor to ground on an ADC pin tells the one satellite firmware image which
board it is running on. Position in the chain is still the address; the kind
only says what the board has on it.

| Kind | Board | Slots |
|---|---|---|
| `1` | potentiometer (`rodeo-pot-1`) | 1 × `POT` |
| `2` | button (`rodeo-button-1`, specified) | 1 × `BUTTON` |
| `3` | encoder with push switch (`rodeo-encoder-1`) | 1 × `ENCODER` |
| `4` | rotary switch, up to 8 positions (`rodeo-rotary-switch-1`, specified) | 1 × `SWITCH` |
| `5` | pot bank ×4 (`rodeo-pot-bank-4`) | 4 × `POT` |
| `6` | encoder bank ×4 (`rodeo-encoder-bank-4`) | 4 × `ENCODER` |
| `7` | button bank ×4 (`rodeo-button-bank-4`) | 4 × `BUTTON` |
| `14` | custom board on its own firmware (the satellite dev board) | 1–4, as it declares |
| `15` | no resistor fitted — reports `EMPTY` | 1 |

Kinds 8–13 are kept for future Rodeo satellites. A new board type needs a
resistor value and a firmware update, not a firmware of its own. Kind 14 is the
exception: a board Rodeo doesn't make runs its own firmware and declares its
slots when the Seed discovers it, so the Seed needs nothing new to read it.

:::note[Kind 7 is written but not yet on a board]
The contract gives `rodeo-button-bank-4` kind 7 and an `RGB` output slot — a
colour as RGB565, which fits the two data bytes every slot already has. The
satellite firmware and the Seed's driver implement both, along with the WS2812
driver; like all the satellite firmware, none of it has run on a board yet.
:::

## Timing

One frame is in the ring at a time. The Seed sends the next after the previous
returns, or after a timeout.

- Ring time ≈ (4 + 4N) × 10 µs + 40 µs per satellite. For thirty one-slot
  satellites that is about **2.5 ms**.
- Default scan period **4 ms**, so 250 scans a second. A control change reaches
  the Seed within roughly one period plus one ring time — about 6.5 ms worst
  case across thirty satellites.
- Scans are paced from a timer, never from the audio callback.

## Power

**What limits the chain is voltage drop, not current.** It is fed from one end
at 3.3 V, every satellite has to work down to 2.7 V, and each link — 150 mm of
28 AWG plus two connector matings, there and back — is about 0.14 Ω carrying
the current of everything past it.

The baseline is **≈6 mA per satellite** including its indicator LED, for thirty
on one feed through a 0.75 A resettable fuse. That puts the last of thirty
roughly 0.4 V down, leaving about 0.2 V of headroom.

You can spend that headroom. A satellite drawing ΔI more than the baseline at
position *p* lowers the far end by ≈0.14 Ω × *p* × ΔI, and the rule is to keep
**Σ (*p* × ΔI) under about 1.4 A** across the whole chain. Four single-colour
lit buttons cost 85 mV even at position thirty, and under 30 mV ten boards in.

LEDs that want more than 3.3 V or tens of milliamps — addressable RGB, meters —
take their LED power from a separate 5 V feed and keep their microcontroller on
the chain, so logic levels do not move. Rev B does not bring 5 V to a connector
yet; that is on the motherboard's list for its next revision.
